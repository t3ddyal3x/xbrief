# Elegance pass — research and plan (2026-10-14)

Operator: "it just feels plain and simple but without the elegance". Tokyo Night / Day stays (their word, earlier
the same day). Built the same day, then replaced by `2026-10-14_rework.md`.

## What the best of this kind do

| who | what it is | the idea worth taking |
|---|---|---|
| Tufte CSS, gwern.net | long-form reading on the web | **Sources and asides sit in the wide right margin, beside the sentence they support, visible without a click.** On a narrow screen they fold back into the text. Sections open with space and small caps, not rules. |
| Particle | an AI news app that clusters many outlets into one story | Each story is a cluster, and **its sources are shown prominently**, not hidden. The summary is grounded in them. |
| Semafor (Semaform, Signals) | news with fact and opinion kept apart | Fixed labelled parts: the news, then the view, then room for disagreement. Opinion is never mixed into the facts. |
| Techmeme | the clustered tech-news front page | The lead item sits on top with related items nested under it. A story's **height**, how much coverage it drew, shows how big it is. |
| Axios | a daily briefing | Sans-serif headlines over a serif body. Bold lead-in phrases that let you skim. Short blocks. |
| Readwise Reader | a reading app | About 65 characters a line, generous margins, one reading surface for every kind of text. |
| Linear | a calm, dense app | Hierarchy from small steps of brightness, faint dividers and one accent, never from loud colour. |
| Butterick, *Practical Typography* | the rules | 45–90 characters a line, 15–25 px body text on screen, line spacing 120–145% of the type size. Fewer fonts, used with care. |

## Why xbrief feels plain
1. **Most of a 2000 px screen is empty.** The brief is a 1,060 px block with about 470 px of nothing on each side.
   The best reading pages use that margin; ours wastes it.
2. **The evidence is hidden.** Each paragraph's posts sit behind `› 9 posts`. The ↗ marks are the only sign that a
   sentence rests on a post, and they say nothing about who or what.
3. **The headlines are generic.** SF Pro Display at heavy weights is the system default and reads like any app.
   The one elegant touch on the page is the italic Iowan *morning*.
4. **No typographic finish.** Numbers in the prose are lining figures, headings break with a lone word on the
   last line, and nothing marks where a section opens beyond its heading.

## The plan, in order
1. **Sources in the margin (the signature).** On a wide screen, each ↗ in a summary becomes a small superscript
   number, and the right margin shows that post beside the sentence: name, @handle and time, its opening line, and
   a link to X. They stack down the margin in reading order. This is the Tufte and gwern layout, applied to
   Particle's prominent sources. The `› N posts` fold stays for the full posts. On a narrow screen the margin
   notes disappear and the ↗ links work as they do now. CSS only (floated into the margin), no script, built on
   the server from the post, never from the writer's words.
   Layout at 2000 px: rail 232 · text about 660 (about 66 characters) · margin 300, which fills about 1,350 px.
2. **Headlines in Iowan.** The masthead, topic headings and thesis claims move to Iowan semibold in the top step
   of the slate ladder, with *morning* in italic beside the day. SF Pro is kept for controls, bylines and labels,
   and mono for times and counts. That's two faces doing the work, as Butterick advises.
3. **Typographic finish.** Old-style figures in the prose (`$900M`, `2028` sit in the line) and lining tabular
   figures in counts and times. Headings balance their lines and paragraphs avoid a lone last word
   (`text-wrap: balance / pretty`). Each topic opens with its first few words in small caps, Tufte's section
   opening. The HYPE band and Claude's view box get a hairline edge and a faint lift, Linear's depth, instead of a
   flat fill.
4. **Size shows weight (Techmeme).** In the rail, a thin bar after each topic's count, its length the topic's share
   of posts, so the big stories stand out without reading numbers. Optional: cut it if it looks busy.

Left alone: the Tokyo palette and its roles, the read-window strip, HYPE as the lead band, Claude's view under its
own label (Semafor's rule, already kept), and Search, History and Sites beyond the type changes.
