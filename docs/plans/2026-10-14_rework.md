# Starting over — research and plan (2026-10-14)

Operator, after three passes on the look: "the entire thing needs a re-work; it's just not a good experience;
please do some reviews online and start over". **Not built yet.** This replaces `2026-10-14_elegance.md`, which
polished a page whose structure was the problem.

## What is wrong (read as the reader, not the designer)
Measured on the 08:44 brief of 2026-10-14 (278 posts):

1. **It takes 15 minutes, and nothing tells you which 2 matter.** 3,450 words: HYPE 156, twenty topics at 33–84
   words each (1,349), twelve theses (2,093). Every topic is the same block of serif prose, so a one-account story
   ("Ledger wallet drains") weighs the same as one six people talked about. Ranking by how many posts mixed the two:
   "Singapore haze" (10 posts, 2 people) sits above "Hot CPI and the Fed" (8 posts, 5 people).
2. **The headings are drawers, not news.** "AI products and companies", "Stocks, dollar, metals and property",
   "Podcasts and life advice". People scan headings and skip the text under any heading that doesn't inform them
   (Nielsen Norman's "layer-cake" reading), so a scan of this page tells you nothing.
3. **The prose retells tweets as minutes.** "@blknoiz06 quoted @0xNairolf saying…": handles inside sentences,
   who-said-what in reporter's voice. The people you follow, in their own words, are hidden behind a fold or turned
   into a margin note of twenty words. Summaries flatten tone, and social posts are where that hurts most (shapeof.ai).
4. **One reading is split four ways.** A left rail repeats the headings, a Theses tab repeats the brief's theses,
   the margin notes repeat the posts, and the controls sit on every page above the contents.

The look was never the main fault. The page is a shorter feed, not a brief.

## What the best of this kind do
| who | what it is | the idea to take |
|---|---|---|
| Nuzzel (2012–21) | the links your follows shared | **Rank by how many of your people talked about it**, and show who they were and what they wrote. |
| Techmeme | the clustered tech front page | A headline that states the news, with the discussion (posts) **beside it**; a big story is visibly bigger. |
| Bloomberg Five Things, Economist Espresso | the morning briefings | **A fixed small number** of stories told in full; the rest one line each. Done in two minutes. |
| Axios | Smart Brevity | Each story answers **what's new** and **why it matters**, in a sentence or two; then the detail. |
| Particle | an AI news app | The story is the unit; sources shown prominently; depth on demand. |
| shapeof.ai (AI design patterns) | summaries and citations | Fidelity over brevity; put the **source's own words** next to the claim; label what the AI wrote. |
| Reeder, Readwise Reader | feed readers | One reading surface; a post shown as a post, not rewritten. |

## The plan
**What the page is**: your feed's front page. What your people talked about while you were away, the biggest
first, said in one line each; open what you care about; their own words beside every story.

1. **A story is one piece of news or one conversation, never a drawer.** The writer returns, per story, a
   `headline` that says what happened (a sentence with a verb, under 12 words), a `gist` of one or two sentences
   (what's new, why it matters; people by name, no @handles, no links), the `key` posts that carry it (one to three)
   and all its `posts`. HYPE is the same shape, a list of stories that always comes first under its heading.
   Theses are unchanged. Old briefs still show: their title as the headline, their paragraph as the gist, the posts
   they pointed at as the key posts.
2. **The page ranks, not the writer**: by how many people (distinct accounts) a story drew, then posts. HYPE and the
   top four are open; every other story is one line, opened in place with a click (no script needed). About 400
   words open, two minutes to read; the rest one click away.
3. **Their words beside the brief's.** Each open story is two columns on a wide screen: the brief's headline and
   gist on the left in Iowan, its key posts on the right as posts (name, @handle, time, the text as written in the
   system sans, its first picture if it has one, a link to X). Serif is what Claude wrote; sans is what they wrote.
   That split is the page's signature. Narrower, the posts sit under the gist. The `› N posts` fold stays as it is.
4. **One page, one bar.** The left rail goes: its contents are the closed lines now. Controls move into a slim bar at
   the top (tabs, search, hours, Brief now); the schedule line moves to History. The Theses tab goes: its write-ups
   open in place under Arguments. The margin notes, numbered marks and ↗ in the text go, replaced by the key posts.
5. **Faces, last and separately.** Each post's picture of its author, read from the same feed reply the job already
   receives (a parse change, so the browser test runs too). Old posts without one show none. A later step on its own.

Kept: the Tokyo Night / Day colours and their roles (no part of a brief has its own colour), Iowan for reading, the
read-window strip, the `› N posts` fold, Claude's view labelled as Claude's, search, History, and the Sites tab
(which gets the top bar and the same post card; its layout stays).

The writer's change costs nothing extra to run: shorter answers (about 900 words of stories instead of 1,349). A
brief written before the change is never written again; it shows in the old-briefs way above.
