# Design review — 2026-10-14

Every page (Brief, Theses, Sites, History, Search) at 2000 px light and dark, 800 px and phone, through a headless
Brave that refuses writes. Screenshots stay in the session's scratchpad. This follows a first pass the same day that
fixed contrast, a headline and run titles, and still looked wrong to the operator. That pass was hygiene: it
worked inside the existing look and never asked whether the look was right.

## First impression
A code editor with a magazine article open in it. A grey-lavender ground, blue text on blue, a sidebar of thirty
monospaced lines, and a column floating in the middle of a 2000 px screen. Nothing on the page invites you to
read it.

## What it says it is
A developer tool's log viewer. What it is: **a private morning edition**. One reader, read once or twice a day,
HYPE first, then the arguments worth weighing, then the rest, with every claim one click from its post.

## Findings
**Break**
- **The surface is an editor theme.** Tokyo Day's `#e1e2e7` is neither paper nor screen. Ink, headings, links and
  meta were four blues. The first pass made each readable, but they still could not rank anything. Both themes.
- **Monospace for words.** Tabs, the contents (thirty topic titles), bylines, the fold lines, the controls and the
  run lists are all in SF Mono. That's forty-odd lines of terminal type around the prose. Mono belongs to numbers
  and times.
- **No front page.** HYPE, Theses and every topic carry the same heading with the same rule under it. HYPE leads by
  order only. Weight says nothing about importance.

**Dull**
- Saturated blue serif headings, each over a hairline: a documentation look.
- Form controls in the style of 2005: grey wells, hairline borders, a blue-outlined button.
- At 2000 px the 1,060 px block has about 470 px of nothing on each side, and the rail and the column don't feel
  anchored to each other.
- The HYPE paragraph runs to 300 words in one block. That's the writer's output, not the page's (noted, not fixed
  here).

**Polish**
- The ↗ after a sentence is 12 px mono and hard to hit.
- The search page has no current tab.
- When the hours list or a date field is empty, it shows the browser's own `yyyy-mm-dd` placeholder.

## A decision for the operator
`ARCHITECTURE.md` says the page "follows the other local pages (Tokyo Night / Day)". Only macro does. Portmantou
is warm graphite with brass, and Hyperlitmus is its own console with a mint accent. Each app's look comes from
what it is. A reading page should get a reading surface.

**The operator's answer:** "tokyo night were the right colors just wanted it to be more coherent". Tokyo stays.
What was wrong was the roles, not the palette: four blues for four jobs. The page now uses macro's mapping, with
text on Tokyo's slate ladder, teal for HYPE only, and blue for focus only. Changes 2 and 3 below stand; change 1
is replaced by this.

## Leave alone
The fold line (`+ 9 posts · @a, @b`), the ↗ built from post ids, Claude's view under its own label, the Theses
page's order (claim, argument, if it holds, view, the post), Iowan Old Style as the reading face, and the
first pass's run titles and warnings-only status.

## Three changes, in order
1. **A paper surface with one accent, Hyperliquid's mint, which marks HYPE and nothing else.** This replaces the
   Tokyo palette. It is structural and worth more than every detail fix together.
2. **Type by role.** SF Pro for headlines and everything you operate, Iowan for what you read, mono only for
   numbers and times.
3. **An edition's front page.** The day as a headline, a strip under it with a tick for every post across the
   window the read covered (HYPE's in mint, so a quiet night, a busy hour or a hole shows at a glance), HYPE as
   the lead band, and sections set apart by space rather than rules.
